## Documentation Style

Keep the writing basic and minimal. State instructions and facts plainly.

- Don't use semicolons in prose. Write two sentences instead.
- Don't use colons as a grammatical tool mid-sentence. A colon is fine right before a code example,
  image, list or table, and after a short bold label (e.g. `**Linux:**` above a command).
- Don't add statements that reassure the reader or sell the design, such as "the Lambda holds no secret
  of its own", "nothing is shared or stored", "this is safe to re-run" or "the recommended way to...".
  Only include what the reader needs to do the task or understand the system.
- Use short noun-phrase headings, e.g. "Panel Functions" rather than "What Each Panel Does", and
  "Wheel Direction Test" rather than "First Test With the Wheels Off the Ground". Don't use questions
  as headings.
- In hardware and tool lists, list the items and links only, without explaining what each one is for.
- Hard-wrap lines to under 120 characters, breaking at sentence or clause boundaries. Long links and
  copy/paste commands that can't be wrapped are the exception.
